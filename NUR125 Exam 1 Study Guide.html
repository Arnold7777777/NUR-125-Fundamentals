📘 Exam 1 Study Guide

Modules 1-3 · test-taking strategy, prioritization, infection & med safety

Straight from your instructor's Exam 1 prep session (Modules 1-3). Most of this is pure ATI strategy — it transfers directly to the Content Mastery exam.

1 · Test-taking strategy

  • Read the stem carefully. Identify the keywords in the first sentence — condition, stage, route.
  • Positive vs negative wording. Positive = find the correct answer. Negative = find the WRONG one ("needs further teaching," "requires intervention").
  • No what-ifs. Use only the information you were given.
  • Client-centered. Interventions focus on the client unless the stem says otherwise.
  • Book world, not real world. Answer from textbook fundamentals, not what your unit actually does.
  • Read ALL the choices and true/false each one.
  • Watch for absolutes — always, never, all — they're rarely correct.
  • Umbrella principle. Pick the answer that covers the most ground.
  • Opposites & odd answers. If three options say roughly the same thing, the odd one out is often correct — and two options that contradict each other often contain the answer.

2 · Prioritization

ABC → Airway, Breathing, Circulation. Maslow → physiological, safety, psychological. Nursing process → Assess, Diagnose, Plan, Implement, Evaluate.
Unstable before stable · acute before chronic · least invasive first · client before equipment.

3 · Stages of infection

  • Incubation — exposed, no symptoms yet.
  • Prodromal — vague symptoms (fatigue, malaise, low-grade fever). MOST CONTAGIOUS.
  • Illness — specific symptoms (high fever, sore throat).
  • Convalescence — recovery.

4 · Key clinical concepts

Inflammation: vasodilation → ↑ blood flow → redness, warmth, swelling. Chemical mediators INCREASE vascular permeability. WBCs INCREASE at the site. (All three are commonly reversed in distractors.)
Chain of infection — break it with hand hygiene, asepsis, vaccines, proper food storage.
Systemic = fever, fatigue, swollen lymph nodes. Localized = redness, swelling, pain at one site.

5 · Safety & documentation

Medication refusal: ASSESS first (ask why) → educate → document → notify the provider.
Medication error: ASSESS the client first → notify → incident report. The incident report is never referenced in the chart.
Post-mortem care: dentures in before rigor mortis · elevate the head of bed slightly · close eyelids gently · do NOT remove tubes if an autopsy is ordered.
C. difficile: contact precautions (gown + gloves for everyone entering, including visitors) · dedicated equipment in the room · SOAP AND WATER, not sanitizer.

6 · The 15 questions she walked you through

Every one of these is in the quiz — filter Source → 📘 Instructor Guides to drill just them.
  • Inflammation teaching → "Vasodilation increases blood delivery to the injury site."
  • Prodromal, needs further teaching → "My child cannot easily spread strep throat." (They're MOST contagious.)
  • Med refusal, first action → Ask the reason for refusal.
  • Idiosyncratic reaction → An unexpected under- or over-reaction; unpredictable, often genetic.
  • 10 mL acetaminophen → 2 teaspoons (1 tsp = 5 mL).
  • Break the chain (SATA) → hand hygiene, surgical asepsis, vaccines, food storage.
  • End-of-life documentation → "Client reports pain as 4 out of 10 after morphine." Objective and measurable.
  • Fever + fatigue + swollen nodes → systemic infection.
  • Most easily infected → the client with chronic disease.
  • C. diff PPE (SATA) → gloves and gown for all, dedicated equipment, soap and water.
  • Post-mortem → dentures in before rigor mortis.
  • Wrong med given, first action → assess for adverse reactions.
  • UAP uses sanitizer after C. diff → intervene; soap and water is required.
  • Dysphagia, first action → check for a liquid or crushable form.
  • Vague symptoms, "coming down with something" → prodromal stage.
