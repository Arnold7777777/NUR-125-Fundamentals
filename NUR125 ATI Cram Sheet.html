⚡ ATI Fundamentals Cram Sheet

The highest-yield facts for the Content Mastery Proctored Assessment

The 8 rules that answer a third of the exam. If you are short on time, learn these first.
  1. ASSESS FIRST. Fall, med error, refusal, new symptom — always assess the client before you notify, document, or fix anything.
  2. ABC, then Maslow. Airway → Breathing → Circulation. Then physiological → safety → psychological.
  3. Unstable before stable, acute before chronic, actual before risk-for.
  4. Least invasive first. Reposition before you suction; bladder scan before you catheterize.
  5. Never delegate assessment, teaching, evaluation, or an unstable client. UAPs get tasks, not judgment.
  6. Absolutes are almost always wrong. always, never, all, only, every.
  7. Self-report is the gold standard for pain. Pain is whatever the client says it is.
  8. Read the stem twice — half of ATI's difficulty is a negatively worded question ("needs further teaching") that you answered positively.

🧴 Infection control — guaranteed points

Stages of infection: Incubation (exposed, no symptoms) → PRODROMAL (vague symptoms, MOST CONTAGIOUS) → Illness (specific symptoms) → Convalescence (recovery).
PrecautionExamplesPPE
StandardEvery client, every timeGloves; add gown/mask/eye protection if splashing is likely
ContactC. difficile, MRSA, VRE, RSV, scabiesGown + gloves; dedicated equipment
DropletInfluenza, pertussis, meningitis, mumps, rubellaSurgical mask within 3–6 ft; private room
AirborneTB, measles, varicella ("My Chicken Hz TB")N95 + negative-pressure room
ProtectiveNeutropenic / transplant clientPositive-pressure room; no fresh flowers, no raw produce
Alcohol hand rub does NOT work on C. difficile or norovirus. Soap and water — the friction physically removes the spores. Also wash (don't rub) whenever hands are visibly soiled.
Order matters. Donning: gown → mask → goggles → gloves. Doffing: gloves → goggles → gown → mask (dirtiest first, and the mask comes off last, outside the room).

💉 Medication safety

Rights: right client (two identifiers — never the room number), drug, dose, route, time, documentation, reason, response, education, refusal. Three checks: pulling it, preparing it, and at the bedside.
Do-not-use abbreviations: QD, QOD, U, IU, MS/MSO₄, trailing zeros (2.0 mg), naked decimals (.5 mg). Write 0.5 mg and 2 mg.
RouteAngle / techniqueWatch for
Intradermal5–15°, bevel up, raise a blebTB test, allergy testing
Subcutaneous45–90°, do NOT aspirateInsulin, heparin — don't massage heparin
Intramuscular90°, ventrogluteal is safest for adultsZ-track for irritating drugs; vastus lateralis for infants
TransdermalRemove the old patch, rotate, wear gloves, date itHeat ↑ absorption
InhalerBronchodilator first, then steroidRinse the mouth after a steroid (thrush)
Insulin: clear before cloudy · only regular insulin goes IV · never mix glargine · rotate sites · never reuse a needle.
Never crush enteric-coated or extended-release. For dysphagia, look for a liquid form first.

Hypoxia: EARLY = restlessness, anxiety, tachycardia, tachypnea. LATE = cyanosis, bradycardia, confusion. Restlessness is the first sign — treat it as hypoxia until proven otherwise.
Suctioning: sterile technique · hyperoxygenate first · ≤10–15 seconds · suction only on the way OUT · rest between passes.
Sterile field: 1-inch border is contaminated · keep it above your waist · in your sight at all times · wet = contaminated.

🗣️ Therapeutic communication

Use theseNever these
Open-ended questions, silence, reflecting, restating, clarifying, offering self, summarizingFalse reassurance ("you'll be fine") · giving advice · "Why" questions (judgmental) · changing the subject · minimizing · defending
SBAR — Situation (what's happening now), Background (relevant history), Assessment (what you think it is), Recommendation (what you want).
Teach-back is how you evaluate learning — have them explain it back or demonstrate it. Never just ask "do you understand?"
Interpreters must be qualified and professional — never a family member, never a child. Look at and speak to the client.

⚰️ End of life

Palliative = any stage, any age, alongside curative treatment. Hospice = prognosis around 6 months or less, curative treatment stopped.
Hearing is believed to be the last sense to go — keep talking to the client.
Post-mortem: dentures in before rigor mortis · elevate the head slightly · close the eyelids gently · do not remove tubes if there's an autopsy or coroner's case.

Formula: Desired ÷ Have × Quantity. IV pump: mL ÷ hr. Drip rate: (volume × drop factor) ÷ minutes.
Logic check: kilograms is always the smaller number than pounds. If you want more than you have on hand, your answer must be more than one unit.
Round only at the end. Leading zero yes (0.5), trailing zero never (2.0).
Delegation cheat: a UAP can do standard, predictable tasks on a stable client — bathing, feeding, positioning, ambulating, vital signs, I&O, weights. They can never assess, teach, evaluate, or take an unstable client. If three options are assessments and one is a task, the task is your answer.
